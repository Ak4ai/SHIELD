S: INT. MEDIEVAL HOME - 1700 YEARS AGO (GIVE OR TAKE)
N: We open in the center of a MEDIEVAL HOME. Quarters are tight. A stone hearth sits in the middle of the room. Next to the hearth sits a MOTHER, ruddy-cheeked and hearty. She is in the middle of a painful labor, attended to by a MIDWIFE. Next to them sits the FATHER - a small bald man, extremely worried. NARRATOR. (V. 0.) You've probably heard many stories that begin a long long time ago, in a land far far away. This one does as well. But I assure you: this is a story you haven't heard before. It is the story of a boy named Fred. And it begins right here. The Mother SCREAMS in pain.
C: NARRATOR
D: You must excuse Fred's mother. You see, back in the middle ages, having a baby was much harder. There were no hospitals, no medicines for pain. The only thing that remains the same today as it was back then, was the father's role in the birth process.
E: CONT'D
C: MOTHER
D: YOU MISERABLE SWINE! LOOK AT WHAT YOU'VE DONE TO ME! ONE NIGHT YOU DRINK ALE AND FEEL ROMANTIC! ONE NIGHT! AND LOOK AT ME! I'M GIVING BIRTH TO AN OXEN!
E: to Father
N: As always, the Father remains calm and nurturing.
C: FATHER
D: My dear wife, I assure you I feel your agony.
C: MOTHER
D: YOU WANT TO FEEL MY AGONY? GO OUTSIDE, PLUCK A TEN POUND POTATO, SWALLOW IT WHOLE, AND MAKE IT COME OUT THE OTHER END!!!.
C: MIDWIFE
D: It is time. You must push, Dear. Push.
N: One last gigantic PUSH. The midwife pulls back holding something. She looks at the yet unseen baby, astonished:
C: MOTHER
D: What is it!? What is wrong?
C: MIDWIFE
D: He is the fattest baby I have ever seen.
E: stunned
N: The parents GASP, relieved. They look down at their newborn child. The BABY is revealed. He really is the absolute fattest baby of all time. Nevertheless, he's adorable. Smiling even. His cheeks are rosy and flushed, just like his mother.
C: FATHER
D: Why does he not cry?
N: They look back down at their smiling newborn. The baby opens his mouth, about to cry. But instead of crying ...
C: BABY
D: Ho, ho, ho.
N: Stunned, the midwife CROSSES herself. The mother picks up her baby. His weight stuns her as well.
C: MOTHER
D: Oh, good Lord. He's perfect in every way. What will we name him?
E: then
C: FATHER
D: My father was called Nicholas.
C: MOTHER
D: Nicholas. Yes. Nicholas Claus. My Saint Nicholas.
N: The parents take in their child, blissful. Suddenly, they hear a RUSTLING in the corner of the room. It causes Father Claus to realize they've forgotten ...
C: FATHER CLAUS
D: Frederick? Would you like to come and meet your brother?
N: Everyone turns to the corner of the room. ANGLE ON FRED For the first time we reveal a scrawny, tow-headed boy in the corner - FREDERICK Claus. Biting his nails, Fred nervously steps forward. Hesitantly, he takes a very heavy Nicholas in his arms. ------------i'ARRA'WR--------- Fred loved Nicholas from the first moment he met him. In fact, that day he made him a promise Fred holds Nicholas up - face to face. He kisses him on the forehead.
C: FRED
D: I promise to be the best big brother in the world, Nicholas.
C: NICHOLAS
D: Ho, ho, ho.
N: The family LAUGHS and coddles the new baby.
C: NARRATOR
D: But Fred's promise is just the beginning of his story. You see, sometimes it's easier to make a promise than it is to keep one.
T: CUT TO:
S: EXT. THE CLAUS HOME - SIX YEARS LATER
N: The family sits around their pine tree filled backyard. It's a MEDIEVAL BIRTHDAY PARTY. Nicholas (now six, and even fatter) is the center of attention, surrounded by friends and gifts. Fred, now twelve, approaches him. Fred holds a GIFT of his own.
C: FRED
D: Happy birthday, Nicholas. I made this for you.
C: NICHOLAS
D: Why thank you, Frederick!
N: Nicholas HUGS Fred.
C: MOTHER CLAUS
D: That was very nice of you, Frederick. Where are you going Nicholas?
E: then, noticing
N: Nicholas has SCOOPED UP all his gifts (including Fred's).
C: NICHOLAS
D: There's a poor orphan name Henry who lives down the road. I'm bringing all my gifts to him. He needs them more than I.
C: MOTHER CLAUS
D: Why Nicholas! Isn't that lovely?!
N: Fred looks confused.
C: FRED
D: Do you really think Henry needs a leather journal with the name Nicholas on the cover?
C: MOTHER CLAUS
D: Oh. Frederick, stop being difficult. It's a saintly thing he did. My little Saint Nicholas! Mother Claus wraps Nicholas in a bear hug. Almost sympathetically, Father Claus PATS Fred on the shoulder.
T: CUT TO:
S: INT. THE CLAUS HOME - MONTHS LATER
N: Fred comes downstairs, wearing a new suit. He looks adorable.
C: NARRATOR
D: And so it went for Fred. Every time he managed to make his parents proud
C: MOTHER CLAUS
D: Why Frederick, don't you look nice?
C: FATHER CLAUS
D: A real gentleman, Son.
C: NARRATOR
D: Nicholas managed to make his parents even prouder. Nicholas plops out of the CHIMNEY. He's wearing a ridiculous RED JUMPSUIT - almost a one-sie.
C: NICHOLAS
D: The door was locked so I decided to drop down the chimney. How fun!
C: MOTHER CLAUS
D: Nicholas! Don't you look dashing!?
N: Fred tilts his head to look at his brother. In truth, he kind of looks like an idiot. ---------- NI.CHOLAS.---------------- 1 sewed it myself. I know money is tight this year so I used the left-over fabric from your drapery, Mother.
C: MOTHER CLAUS
D: Well, you look just lovely in red. It's definitely your color. Making your own clothes! You really are too much, Nicholas.
N: Mother Claus runs to Nicholas and wraps him in another hug. Fred has busied himself LOOKING UP the chimney.
C: FRED
D: If the door was locked, why didn't you just knock?
C: MOTHER CLAUS
D: Because he's inventive, Frederick! You know, you really should try and be more like your brother.
N: Father Claus PATS Fred on the shoulder once again and follows his wife toward Nicholas.
T: CUT TO:
S: EXT. THE CLAUS HOME - DAYTIME
N: We pan up a large pine tree in a field. At the top, Fred sits on a branch next to a self-made BIRDHOUSE. The birdhouse is bright blue with yellow stripes. Intricate. And feeding in the house is a beautiful BLUEBIRD.
C: FRED
D: I don't wish to hate him. He means well. And I promised to be the best big brother in the world.
E: to bird
N: The bird looks at Fred, his head askew.
C: FRED
D: You're the only one who understands me, Chirp-Chirp.
E: CONT'D
N: The bluebird CHIRPS twice. Fred smiles. But then he hears something: a SAWING NOISE. And just like that ... the tree (with Fred in it) WIPES the frame. ON THE GROUND ---wheJSe----F-Jee NICHOLAS
